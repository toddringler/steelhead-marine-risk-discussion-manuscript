\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
    \item set the premise of the discussion paper.
    \item define the conservation question and scope.
    \item introduce the life-history framework as the basis for analysis.
    \begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic{enumi}\alph*., leftmargin=*]
        \item allows mortality risk to be associated with specific life-history stages.
        \item naturally allows a partitioning and, thus, comparison, of the freshwater, estuary, and marine life-history risks.
        \item seamlessly accounts for stage-specific risks, carryover risks, and cumulative risk.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item explain how the life-history framework informs this analysis.
    \begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic{enumi}\alph*., leftmargin=*]
        \item used as the structure for organizing and interpreting conservation efforts.
        \item for example, NGO conservation projects can be associated with specific life-history stages.
        \item similarly, actions by government agencies--local, state, provincial, federal, international--can also be associated with specific life-history stages.
        \item ESA listing actions can also be associated with specific life-history stages.
        \item associating conservation actions with specific life-history stages allows for an evaluation of whether or not conservation effort is well aligned with the life-history risks.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item outline the structure of the discussion paper.
    \begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic{enumi}\alph*., leftmargin=*]
        \item life-history stages and associated risks will be introduced and briefly described.
        \item NGO, government agency, and ESA listing actions are associated with specific life-history stages.
        \item structural mismatches are identified and discussed.
        \item plausible explanations for the observed mismatches are explored.
        \item in the spirit of a discussion paper, potential follow-on questions are raised.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
